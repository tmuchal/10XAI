Harness Theater · YouTube long-form · storyboard + full script

14-Minute Deep DiveOntology + harness engineering, explained on a multi-agent Kanban board

A viral post promises a full app from one prompt, for free. Dr. Harness, a hip hamster in sunglasses, pastes it into 10XAI and walks the viewer through why prompts alone break, what a harness is, what an ontology is, and how a crew of AI agents turns the post into claimed-versus-measured numbers. His co-star Uchu (우츄) believes every viral post and is shocked by every reveal. The Kanban board builds up one layer per chapter, and card #4 is the cliffhanger.

Running order

Each block is one shot, sized by its screen time and colored by chapter. Click a block to play the animatic from that shot. The animatic uses the real shot durations, so it runs the full length of the episode.

Cast

Two leads and a crew. The hamster explains, Uchu reacts, and the agents do the work on the board.

Dr. Harness

Host and narrator: a hip golden hamster in black sunglasses, a backwards snapback, a gold 10X chain, an oversized hoodie and sneakers. Deadpan, quick, a little smug. Pushes the sunglasses up only for reveals. Speaks the English VO; Korean captions in 해요체.

Uchu (우츄)

Co-star and audience stand-in: the viewer who believes every viral post. Red hooded suit, round ear pods, antenna stalks holding fuzzy red "uchu" lettering, a green face and a big shocked O mouth. Always shocked while the hamster stays deadpan. Short English lines; Korean captions in 반말. Appears in S01, S02, S05, S16, S27, S28, S33 and S34, plus thumbnail D.

The crew

Orchestrator, Decompose, Gap-fill, Verify, Router, Exec runner, Repair and Deploy, colored to match agents/*.md. They speak in "boop" gibberish.

Thumbnail and title

Four thumbnail concepts for A/B testing with YouTube's Test and Compare. Each one uses a single short hook, a character face (the hamster, or Uchu's shock face in D), and one red number. The Blender hero stills will replace these sketches once they're rendered.

Title options

Titles are in English for the English VO. The Korean line is a gloss for the team, and it doubles as the Korean title if the video gets a localized title.

    Korean subtitle style
    
    
    The VO is English. The Korean line under it is written as a native Korean YouTube caption, for meaning, not word for word. The on-frame lines in the shot cards already follow these rules; the full Korean caption text in each VO field gets split at clause breaks into lines of the same length.
    
    
    
      
    Rules
      
        
    • 18 characters or fewer per line, spaces included. Netflix's Korean timed-text guide caps lines at 16, so aim for 16 when you can.
      • 
        
    • Conversational 해요체 throughout, the explainer register.
      • 
        
    • Rewrite for meaning. Drop subjects and pronouns when the picture already says who.
      • 
        
    • No 번역투: no "~하는 것은", "~에 의해", "그것은", "~에 대해", "~을 가지고 있다", and no passives.
      • 
        
    • Use the terms Korean creators actually say: 에이전트, 칸반, 샌드박스, 하네스, 온톨로지, 게이트.
      • 
        
    • Mark the key token with asterisks in both lines (*70*, *무료*, *SKILL.md*). Theater.sub2 highlights it in color.
      • 
        
    • Uchu speaks 반말 in Korean ("공짜라며?!", "그냥 돌려!"). The narration stays in 해요체.
      • 
        
    • The hamster's signature "~햄" ending appears on four punch lines only: S01, S17, S28 and S34.
      •
