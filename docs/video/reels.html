30-Second ReelIt said FREE. We measured.

A viral post promises a free AI agent in five minutes. Dr. Harness, a hip hamster in sunglasses, a backwards cap and a gold "10X" chain, pastes it onto the 10XAI Kanban board. A team of AI agents cuts it into cards, tags every card, pumps up a risk score, holds the risky card at the gate and times the safe ones in a sandbox. His foil is Uchu (우츄), the always-shocked fan who believes every viral post. The Reel opens on the payoff, ends on a mic drop and loops back to its first line.

Why this works

Research behind the cut

  • Payoff first. OpusClip's Shorts hook formulas open on a claim or a contradiction. R01 answers its own headline: a deadpan hamster holds up FREE, struck out, over $0.40, in front of the Kanban board, while Uchu's shocked O face is the pattern interrupt. R02 shows the agent squad that measured it, then R03 rewinds.
  • Three scene changes in three seconds. Nielsen's Visual Attention work links 3 or more scene changes in the first 3 seconds with 58% higher completion. R01 (hamster verdict), R02 (agent squad) and R03 (rewind) are one-second micro-shots with three different pictures.
  • Hold past second ten. Meta reports that 65% of people who watch 3 seconds of a video reach 10 seconds. The board is on screen from frame 1, and by 0:06 the agents are throwing cards into its columns (R05).
  • A change every 2 to 4 seconds. After the hook, every shot runs 2 to 3 seconds, and each one adds a new prop or a new agent.
  • One metaphor per concept. Ontology is name tags on a card (R07). Harness is seatbelts for agents (R08a), then its four parts are numbered on screen: rules and tests (R08b ②), the gate (R09 ③) and the sandbox (R10 ④).
  • Loop ending. The last line, "Next free post?" / "다음 공짜 글, 재볼까?", runs straight into "It said FREE." / "공짜라며?" in both languages. A seamless loop turns the end of the video into a replay.
  • Safe zones. Key text sits inside logical x 30–480 and y 110–765 (1080×1920: x 60–960, y 220–1530), clear of the top bar, the caption area and the icon column. The guides follow outfy, kreatli and veeso.
  • Two languages at once. English VO with a burned-in EN + KR subtitle strip, so the Reel works with the sound off in both markets. The Korean line is written as a native Shorts caption in the hamster's own voice, so it carries the meaning in its own words.

Korean subtitle style

  • Voice. The hamster talks in 반말 with 음슴체 punch lines ("채움", "잡음", "까봄"). No 해요체 mixed in.
  • Signature ending. "~햄" appears twice (R02 "재봤햄", R10 "돌렸햄"), plus once in the mascot hook alternative. Like 뉴닉's 고슴체, the ending becomes the brand, so it is never on every line.
  • Length. 16 Korean characters or fewer, one line per language.
  • Key tokens. *Asterisks* mark the same key word in both lines: FREE/공짜, agents/에이전트, Ontology/온톨로지, Harness/하네스, Tests/테스트, 70/70점, sandboxed/샌드박스, $0.40. Theater.sub2 draws them in a highlight color at 1.12× size.
  • Meaning over wording. Each Korean line says the point in its own words. Subjects and pronouns are cut. No passives, no "~에 대해", no "~을 가지고 있다".
  • Terms. Keep the forms Korean creators use: 칸반, 에이전트, 샌드박스, 온톨로지, 하네스, SKILL.md.
  • References. 조코딩, 안될공학, ITSub잇섭, 뉴닉 고슴이, 빵빵이의 일상, 영국남자, 올리버쌤.

Before you animate

  • Placeholder numbers. RISK 72, $0.40, 12 min, 8 cards and the R10 rolling counters are placeholders. Replace them with the numbers from your own dry run before the final render (CLAUDE.md rule 5: claimed vs. measured).
  • Facts on screen. Cards hold at RISK_GATE_THRESHOLD = 70. Verify runs on Claude + Codex. Pasting goes through POST /api/ingest. Each run gets its own git worktree. Card tags come from metadata: kind, claim, measured, risk, gate.
  • Subtitle length. English lines are 33 visible characters or fewer and Korean lines 16 or fewer. The strip is drawn at 22/20 px logical and centered on the safe zone (x 255), so it stays inside x 30–480.
  • Sound. Ukulele and toy piano at about 110 BPM, with a record-scratch rewind at 0:02, a kazoo sting on the reveal at 0:23 and a mic thud at 0:27.

Blender motion plan

The final Reel is 3D motion graphics made in Blender. The SVG frames on this page are the 2D storyboard and animatic. Each shot card has a Blender field with its camera move, depth layers and motion graphics.

Scenes and sets

  • SET_Stage: the sunburst backdrop, red curtains and honey-wood floor as one toon-shaded set. Every shot lives here.
  • SET_Board: four 3D Kanban columns (Decomposed, Verifying, Gate / Review, Verified) that can flip on their long axis.
  • SET_Gate: the striped barrier, the rubber stamp and the RISK meter with its pump.
  • SET_Sandbox: a sand tray with a glass lid, the stopwatch and the cost coin with rolling counters.
  • PROP_Harness: belts and one shared strap for the agents (R08a), the RULES scroll and the TESTS bell fence (R08b).
  • PROP_Phone: the viral post, with a screen texture the camera can fly through.

Hamster rig

  • Rigify base with a squash-and-stretch body and bendy arms. Costume: purple hoodie (cloth sim), backwards red snapback, gold "10X" chain on a spring bone, chunky sneakers.
  • Face shape keys: smile, grin, O-mouth, flat, cheeks_full (pouch stuffing), blink.
  • Sunglasses on their own bone, with a "glasses_up" pose for reveals (R11). Deadpan face (flat mouth, one slow blink) for R01, R04, R06 and R11. Hand poses: sign grip (R01), strap yank (R08a), stamp raised (R09), mic drop and paw press on glass (R12).
  • Ear flicks and whiskers on a jiggle modifier.
  • Uchu: red hooded suit with ear pods, antenna stalks on a jiggle chain (the fuzzy "uchu" lettering as a hair/curve object), and face shape keys for shock (big O), grin, smile and squint, plus a sweat-drop particle. Uchu reacts in R01, R11 and R12.
  • Agent boxes: one shared box rig with a mood face swap and prop sockets.

Camera language

  • Hook shots: snap zooms and whip-pans, 24–35 mm.
  • Board shots: slow dolly and orbit at 50 mm with shallow depth of field on the active card.
  • Transitions: fly through the rewind glyph (R03→R04), fly through the phone screen (R04→R05), the card lifting out of the board (R07), board flip to the Gate column (R08b→R09), board flip to Verified (R11→R12).
  • Fourth-wall beat: in R12 the hamster walks into the lens and presses a paw on a glass plane at the near clip.
  • Three depth layers in every shot: curtains in front, cast in the middle, board or backdrop behind, for parallax.

Render settings

  • EEVEE, 24 fps, Filmic off, Standard view transform for bright saturated color.
  • Toon shader (Shader to RGB with a two-step ramp) plus Line Art outlines in ink #3A2418, to match the storyboard.
  • Grease Pencil for hand-drawn accents: speed lines, DING marks, the stamp, paw prints.
  • Particles for confetti, sand and card snips. Rigid body for cards on physics arcs.
  • Subtitles and hook type as kinetic text objects, locked to camera.

Output per format

  • Reel / Short: 1080×1920, 24 fps, H.264, about 16 Mbps, 30 s (720 frames). Burned-in EN + KR subtitles. Loop point at frame 720.
  • Cover: R01 as a 1080×1920 PNG, checked against the 3:4 grid crop.
  • Long-form: the same scenes re-framed at 1920×1080.
  • Card news: hero stills at 1080×1350.
