45-Second ReelIt said FREE. We measured.

A viral post promises a free AI agent in five minutes. Dr. Harness, a hamster scientist in sunglasses, pastes it onto the 10XAI Kanban board, where a team of AI agents cuts it into cards, tags every card, pumps up a risk score, holds the risky card at the gate, and times the safe ones in a sandbox. The Reel opens on the payoff and loops back to its first line.

Runtime 0:45 Shots 15 Canvas 1080×1920 (logical 540×960, 9:16) VO English · subtitles EN + KR Production Blender 3D

Running order

Fifteen shots in seven beats. Each block is as wide as its shot is long. The first three shots last one second each, so the viewer sees three different frames before second three. Click a block to play from that shot.

Storyboard

Each card lists the camera, the action, the English voice-over with its Korean subtitle, the text on screen, the sound, the 2D animation notes and the Blender 3D plan. Click a frame to play the animatic from there. Turn on "Show safe zones" to check each frame against the Reels and Shorts interface.

Hook alternatives

Three other first seconds to test against R01. Each one opens on a result, then rewinds with the same R03 shot, so the rest of the cut stays the same.

Why this works

Research behind the cut

  • Payoff first. OpusClip's Shorts hook formulas open on a claim or a contradiction. R01 shows the red card hitting the gate with RISK 72 under the words "IT SAID FREE", then R03 rewinds.
  • Three scene changes in three seconds. Nielsen's Visual Attention work links 3 or more scene changes in the first 3 seconds with 58% higher completion. R01, R02 and R03 are one-second micro-shots.
  • Hold past second ten. Meta reports that 65% of people who watch 3 seconds of a video reach 10 seconds. By 0:10 the agents are already moving cards (R05, R06).
  • A change every 2 to 4 seconds. After the hook, every shot runs 3 to 4 seconds, and each one adds a new prop or a new agent.
  • Loop ending. The last line, "So paste the next post…", runs straight into "It said FREE." A seamless loop turns the end of the video into a replay.
  • Safe zones. Key text sits inside logical x 30–480 and y 110–765 (1080×1920: x 60–960, y 220–1530), clear of the top bar, the caption area and the icon column. The guides follow outfy, kreatli and veeso.
  • Two languages at once. English VO with a burned-in EN + KR subtitle strip, so the Reel works with the sound off in both markets.

Before you animate

  • Placeholder numbers. RISK 72, $0.40, 12 min and 8 cards are placeholders. Replace them with the numbers from your own dry run before the final render (CLAUDE.md rule 5: claimed vs. measured).
  • Facts on screen. Cards hold at RISK_GATE_THRESHOLD = 70. Verify runs on Claude + Codex. Pasting goes through POST /api/ingest. Each run gets its own git worktree. Card tags come from metadata: kind, claim, measured, risk, gate.
  • Subtitle length. English lines are 36 characters or fewer, so the strip stays inside the safe zone at 24 px logical.
  • Sound. Ukulele and toy piano at about 110 BPM, with a record-scratch rewind at 0:02 and a kazoo sting on the reveal at 0:35.

Blender motion plan

The final Reel is 3D motion graphics made in Blender. The SVG frames on this page are the 2D storyboard and animatic. Each shot card has a Blender field with its camera move, depth layers and motion graphics.

Scenes and sets

  • SET_Stage: the sunburst backdrop, red curtains and honey-wood floor as one toon-shaded set. Every shot lives here.
  • SET_Board: four 3D Kanban columns (Decomposed, Verifying, Gate / Review, Verified) that can flip on their long axis.
  • SET_Gate: the striped barrier, the rubber stamp and the RISK meter with its pump.
  • SET_Sandbox: a sand tray with a glass lid, the stopwatch and the sunflower-seed coin.
  • SET_Wheel: the hamster wheel that powers the 10XAI machine (the harness gag).
  • PROP_Phone: the viral post, with a screen texture the camera can fly through.

Hamster rig

  • Rigify base with a squash-and-stretch body and bendy arms.
  • Face shape keys: smile, grin, O-mouth, flat, cheeks_full (pouch stuffing), blink.
  • Sunglasses on their own bone, with a "glasses_up" pose for reveals (R13).
  • Ear flicks and whiskers on a jiggle modifier.
  • Agent boxes: one shared box rig with a mood face swap and prop sockets.

Camera language

  • Hook shots: snap zooms and whip-pans, 24–35 mm.
  • Board shots: slow dolly and orbit at 50 mm with shallow depth of field on the active card.
  • Transitions: fly through the rewind glyph (R03→R04), fly through the phone screen (R04→R05), wheel-spin wipe (R08→R09), board flip (R13→R14).
  • Three depth layers in every shot: curtains in front, cast in the middle, board or backdrop behind, for parallax.

Render settings

  • EEVEE, 24 fps, Filmic off, Standard view transform for bright saturated color.
  • Toon shader (Shader to RGB with a two-step ramp) plus Line Art outlines in ink #3A2418, to match the storyboard.
  • Grease Pencil for hand-drawn accents: speed lines, DING marks, the stamp, paw prints.
  • Particles for confetti, seed husks and card snips. Rigid body for cards on physics arcs.
  • Subtitles and hook type as kinetic text objects, locked to camera.

Output per format

  • Reel / Short: 1080×1920, 24 fps, H.264, about 16 Mbps, 45 s. Burned-in EN + KR subtitles. Loop point at frame 1080.
  • Cover: R01 as a 1080×1920 PNG, checked against the 3:4 grid crop.
  • Long-form: the same scenes re-framed at 1920×1080.
  • Card news: hero stills at 1080×1350.
